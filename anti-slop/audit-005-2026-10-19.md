## Findings

### 23. MEDIUM: mobile anchors land under the sticky header

At 390px, following the hero's own buttons leaves the section heading behind the header:

| Anchor | Target top | Header bottom | Heading | Heading covered |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `#drop` | 0 | 114px | `H2 The drop` at y=89 | **25px** |
| `#how-it-works` | 0 | 114px | `H2 How Ordering Works` at y=65 | **49px (hidden: the h2 is ~26px tall)** |

At 1280px the same anchors clear the 65px header by 55px and 32px, so this is mobile only.
Nothing compensates for the header: `scroll-margin-top` computes to `0px` on both sections, and
`html`'s `scroll-padding-top` computes to `auto`. Reproduced on a direct load of `/#drop` and on
an in-page click, so a shared link and the hero button both land wrong.

This is the layoutmobile "Sticky Nav Steals the Screen" pattern, and it breaks the project's own
rule at `DESIGN.md:272` ("don't let a fixed element cover content at 360px"). The header is 114px
on phones because it holds the 64px logo row plus the 49px closed "Menu" disclosure row.

Fix, one declaration in `src/styles/global.css`:

```css
html {
  scroll-padding-top: 7.5rem;
}
```

120px clears the 114px mobile header with a little air, and it fixes every current and future
in-page anchor at once. The alternative, `scroll-mt-28` on the two home sections, fixes only
these two.

### 24. MEDIUM: touch targets under 44px, several of them 4px apart

Measured at 320, 390 and 768. The counts are instances across the eight routes at those three
widths, so "x24" means eight routes times three widths. Sizes are the element's own box, after
checking for a wrapping label or an enlarging pseudo-element (see the pass list at the end: the
header icons and the payment radios are covered by `::before` and by their label, so they are not
in this list).

| Control | Own box | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Footer list links (8 labels, 58x15 to 104x15) | 15px tall, e.g. 70x15 | every route, x24 |
| Header wordmark | 118x20 | every route, x24 |
| Breadcrumb links | 31x17 to 79x17 | cart, checkout, pdp, x18 |
| Cart "Remove" | 48x17 | cart, x3 |
| "Edit cart" | 66x17 | checkout, x3 |
| "Fit Guide (Inches / CM)" | 139x17 | pdp, x3 |
| Size chips S, M, L, XL, 2XL | 32x34 to 47x34 | pdp, x3 |
| Contact phone, email, Facebook links | 88x15, 175x15, 181x15 | contact, x3 |
| "Browse Drop 001" | 171x40 | cart empty state |
| "Select a print" | 162x36 | checkout |
| "Close size guide" (the dialog's only control) | 31x40 | pdp, size guide |

The footer links are also 4px apart vertically: their inline boxes are 15px tall inside a 12px
`gap-3`, so the clickable strip ends and the next begins almost immediately. That is the
layoutmobile "Targets Too Close" case, and the same fix solves both: `min-h-11` (or `py-3`) on
the footer, breadcrumb, cart meta and contact links, and `min-h-11` on the size chips.

`DESIGN.md:272` already states the rule ("don't ship a control below a 44px touch target"), so
these are the site's own standard being missed rather than an outside preference.

### 25. MEDIUM: control boundaries are below the 3:1 non-text threshold

Computed against the surface each control sits on:

| Control | Border | On | Ratio | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Text fields, textarea, province select (all routes) | `#e2d8c8` | paper `#f4ede2` | **1.21:1** | 3:1 |
| Unselected size chips | `#e2d8c8` | paper | **1.21:1** | 3:1 |

Two states do pass, and they are worth naming so the finding is not read as broader than it is:
the selected chip carries `border-ink` on an ink fill (and sits on paper at 16.25:1), and the
filled buttons `.btn-primary` and `.btn-invert` have borders the same colour as their own fill, so
their boundary to the page is 16.25:1. The failure is the resting hairline.

`--color-line` at `#e2d8c8` is a texture choice, not a boundary. If the boundary is to meet 3:1,
the first candidate that clears it on all three surfaces it appears on:

| Token | On paper | On the `bg-white` cards | On the `line/20` band |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#e2d8c8` (current) | 1.21 | 1.41 | 1.17 |
| `#9e917f` | 2.65 | 3.08 | 2.55 |
| `#8d8073` | **3.30** | **3.84** | **3.18** |

That is a visibly darker hairline, so it is the owner's call: it is the one finding here that
changes the look of the site rather than fixing a defect. Text contrast is unaffected and passes
everywhere (below).

### 26. MEDIUM: every text-entry control is 14px, so iOS zooms the page on focus

The computed font size of the entry controls, measured on cart and checkout:

- `input` fields (name, email, phone, street, city, zip, payment reference): **14px**
- `select` (province): **14px**
- `textarea` (order note): **14px**

Safari on iOS zooms the viewport when a control under 16px receives focus, and it does not zoom
back out. The `field` class in `checkout.astro` and the matching classes in `cart.astro` are
`text-sm`. Fix: `text-base sm:text-sm`, which keeps the desktop look and gives phones a 16px
control. The payment radios are already 16px, and the buttons (12px) are not affected because
buttons do not receive a software keyboard.

### 27. LOW: the two header icon hit boxes overlap by 8px

At every width up to 768, on every route: the contact link's box is 20x20 expanded by
`before:-inset-3` to 44x44, and the cart button is 22x22 expanded to 46x46. With `gap-4` (16px)
between the 20px and 22px icons, the expanded boxes overlap by 8px horizontally and 45px
vertically. A thumb aimed at the edge of one can hit the other. `gap-4` to `gap-7` on that row in
`Header.astro` clears it.

### 28. LOW: phones hold 114px of sticky chrome

The header is 114px at 320 and 390 (64px logo row + 49px closed "Menu" row), which is 17% of a
667px phone screen, held permanently. At 1024 and up it drops to 65px because the disclosure row
is `lg:hidden`. The "Menu" row is a closed disclosure most of the time, so 49px of it is spent on
a control the user has not opened. Option: move the disclosure below the sticky row, so only the
64px row sticks.

### 29. LOW: the 11px label family does not follow the user's font size

Setting the root font size to 32px (a 200% text resize) doubles 39 of 68 text elements on home,
38 of 66 on checkout and 33 of 48 on the PDP. The rest stay pinned: 29, 28 and 15 elements
respectively, almost all at `11px`, plus two at `13px` on the PDP. Those are the `text-[11px]`
values in `.meta`, `.label`, the header nav, the footer, the badge, the fit-guide trigger and the
cart drawer, and the `text-[13px]` item titles in `ProductCard`, `CartDrawer`, `cart.astro` and
`checkout.astro`.

Nothing breaks: at 200% text there is **no horizontal overflow and no pan** on any of the three
pages. The labels simply ignore the preference, because they are px rather than rem. `text-[0.6875rem]`
and `text-[0.8125rem]` make them scale. Polish, not a defect.

### 30. LOW: on mobile the checkout slip is below the submit button

`checkout.astro` puts the form first and the order slip (`aside`, items, delivery, total) second,
in a grid that only becomes two columns at `lg`. On a phone the sequence is therefore: address
fields, terms, **Send Order Request**, then the totals. The user is asked to submit before seeing
what they are submitting. The layoutmobile skill's own wording is that order should change "where
the content needs it". `order-first lg:order-none` on the aside puts the slip above the form on
phones only.

## Checked and passing

These are the reasons the checklist answers below are clean, so they are evidence rather than
commentary.

**No horizontal overflow, any width, any route.** 74 page-loads: 320, 360, 390, 414, 600, 768
(7 routes each) and 834, 1024, 1280, 1440 (8 routes each). Document `scrollWidth` equals the
viewport in every case, and a scroll attempt (`scrollTo(600, 0)`) leaves `scrollX` at 0 in every
case. Zero elements extend past the viewport without a clipping ancestor.

**The one clipped container is the marquee,** `div.overflow-hidden.border-y`, cutting 1880 to
1974px of its own track at each width. That is the second copy of the loop, which is how a
seamless marquee is built and what `DESIGN.md` records; the band is crop by design, not hidden
content.

**Grids reflow in real steps, not two states.** Product grid: 2 tracks of 136px at 320, 156 at
360, 171 at 390, 183 at 414, 276 at 600, then 3 tracks of 251px at 834, 304 at 1024, 390 at 1280,
395 at 1440. The drop grid on home is 1 column, then 2 at 640, then 3 at 1024 (294 to 384px
tracks). The footer is 1 column, 2 at 640, 4 at 1024. No track is squeezed and none collides at
320: the smallest card, 136px at 320, still fits its 13px title and its price plus size row.

**Text contrast passes everywhere.** 0 failures across 390 and 1280 on all 7 routes, with 0
colours the parser could not read. The sweep composites alpha text over the real background chain
and understands the `oklab()` values Chrome computes for Tailwind v4's opacity utilities, so
`text-paper/70`, `text-ink/75` and `text-muted` on the `line/20` band were all included. The
tightest pairings, from the formula:

| ink on paper, paper on ink | 16.25 | PASS | PASS |
| muted `#6d6459` on paper | 4.99 | PASS | PASS |
| registration `#c8202a` on paper | 4.89 | PASS | PASS |
| paper on registration (marquee, red buttons) | 4.89 | PASS | PASS |

| muted on the white cards | 5.81 | PASS | PASS |

**Focus is visible at every stop.** 48 Tab stops, 16 each on home, the PDP and the cart, with
transitions disabled so the colours are final: 42 stops carry the 2px `#c8202a` outline on paper
(4.89:1), 2 carry it on ink (3.33:1) and 4 use the 1px registration ring on paper (4.89:1). The
run stopped before checkout and contact, so those two routes were not swept for focus; audit-004's
click-through does exercise checkout's controls and both dialogs. A first pass sampled rings
mid-transition and reported 2.63:1 on the dark hero; with the transition settled it is 3.33:1.
Report the settled colour.

**Hit boxes that are enlarged on purpose.** The contact icon (20x20 to 44x44) and cart button
(22x22 to 46x46) expand with `before:-inset-3`; the payment radios (13x13) and the province
selects (39px tall) inherit their wrapping label, giving 62 to 69px tall targets. These are
compliant and are not counted in finding 24.

**The size-guide dialog behaves.** Opening it moves focus to `Close size guide`, locks body
scroll (`overflow: hidden` on the body), closes on Escape, and returns focus to the `Fit Guide`
button that opened it. Its single control is undersized (finding 24), but the keyboard path is
intact. Measured with the trigger focused first: a programmatic click alone leaves focus on the
body, which would have produced a false finding.

**The 44px controls that are already right.** Quantity steppers are 44x44 (`h-11 w-11`) on the
PDP and in cart rows, the mobile menu rows are 44px minimum, and the tap targets that were
verified working in audit-004 (skip link, drawer, Escape, stepper, chips) still behave.

## Harness notes, for the next run

Three things cost time and are worth writing down, since none of them was a site defect:

- **Same-document navigation never fires a load event.** Waiting on `Page.loadEventFired` while
  changing only a hash, or reloading the same URL, stalls for the full timeout. Poll
  `document.readyState` instead. This is what made one run appear to hang.
- **Chrome computes Tailwind v4 opacity colours as `oklab()`.** A parser that only understands
  `rgb()` silently skips them, which quietly removed `text-paper/70` style elements from the first
  contrast sweep. The final sweep converts OKLab and reports 0 unparsed colours.
- **One Chrome tab appears to degrade over many navigations.** The runs with a fresh profile and a
  fresh target were fast (100ms page loads); the long-lived ones stalled to 30 seconds a page. The
  exact cause was not isolated, but a fresh profile and a fresh target per phase fixed it, and that
  is what the finished numbers come from.

`python` is not installed on this machine, so the contrast checker script could not be run; the
formula and the reference table it ships with were used instead, and the DOM sweep implements the
same formula.
